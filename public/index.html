01 · The firm

A power investment firm built for the part of the market that has no natural owner.

Venture capital funds the physics. Project finance funds the proven. Between them sits the first commercial plant, the first factory, the first fleet — where the technology already works and the only remaining question is whether it can be built.

Inertia Fund is a specialized investment platform focused on the global power sector. Its research covers the whole value chain — from early technology, through the equipment suppliers and construction platforms, to regulated utilities and on-site generation — because a supplier's order book is a leading indicator for a developer's schedule, and a technology's failure mode is a repricing signal for an operating asset. Its capital is concentrated where that visibility becomes an advantage: the segment between proven technology and bankable infrastructure, where technical judgment is worth the most and is least available.

We are not a climate fund, a data-center fund, or an artificial-intelligence fund. Electricity demand is growing for many reasons in many regions, and artificial intelligence is roughly a tenth of that growth. The firm is built to be right about the other nine tenths.

We judge a project on whether it can be built on time, not on how good the idea sounds.

We will pay the right price for a shortage we have verified ourselves.

We will not pay for a shortage everybody already knows about, because that scarcity is already in the price.

Most investors in this market ask whether the technology works and whether someone will buy the output. We ask a third question, and we ask it first: can this be delivered, on the date promised, at the price promised? That question is answered by procurement, contractors, permits and equipment deliveries — not by the technology. It is the question our team has spent careers answering.

02 · Where we invest

Four segments of one system.

Capital is matched to the need. The toolkit runs from control and minority equity, through growth equity and hybrid instruments, to project-level and development equity — chosen after we have priced the remaining execution risk, not before.

  • Early technology and disruptors

    Technologies that remove a physical bottleneck: getting more out of the grid already in the ground, stretching or replacing sold-out machines, firm power that can sit near the customer, and tools that shorten the queue. A technology that does none of these is a science story, not an Inertia Fund story.

    Growth and structured equity

  • Suppliers, construction and platforms

    The equipment and construction layer beneath the largest manufacturers — forgings, castings, transformer components, instrumentation, module fabrication, specialist contractors — where the shortage is real and not yet in the price.

    Control and significant minority

  • Utilities and contracted assets

    Generation and network assets with long contracts and creditworthy buyers, including co-investment alongside larger infrastructure platforms once an asset has been de-risked.

  • On-site and industrial power

03 · What we believe

Twelve theses, each written so that it can be proved wrong.

Every thesis carries a dated, observable condition that would make us abandon it, and a schedule on which we check. A view everyone holds is a description of the market, not an edge in it.

  1. 01Delivering electricity on time is the core problem, and the choke point has moved from the power plant to the grid connection.
  2. 02The state has become a counterparty in every transaction, and public consent has become an input to the capital stack.
  3. 03The first commercial plant has no natural owner. The institution built to fund it withdrew in early 2026.
  4. 04The scarce equipment and construction layer sits below the largest manufacturers, where the shortage is not yet in the price.
  5. 05On-site power began as a way around the grid and is becoming a regulated, market-participating asset with duties owed to it.
  6. 06Firm and flexible capacity is short, and a high price alone has repeatedly failed to create more of it.
  7. 07A promise to use less power is becoming something that can be bought, sold and financed — and it is underpriced.
  8. 08New technology earns its place only by removing one of four bottlenecks: speed, capacity, reliability at a place and time, or scarce inputs.
  9. 09Demand is rising in many regions for many reasons. Artificial intelligence is about a tenth of it.
  10. 10Electricity demand and equipment demand are different variables with different volatilities. We are paid on the second.
  11. 11In every case, the buyer's creditworthiness is the investment. Who signs, and are they good for it for fifteen years?
  12. 12We will not pay for a shortage that is already in the price.

05 · The name

Inertia is a technical term before it is a metaphor.

In a power system, inertia is the kinetic energy stored in the rotating mass of large generators. It is what holds the frequency of the grid steady in the seconds after a plant trips, before any control system can react. As inverter-based resources displace spinning machines, that stored energy declines — and its decline is one of the defining reliability problems of this decade.

The firm takes its name from a 2023 paper on exactly that problem, written by members of its founding group.

Capital with mass — deployed by people who have built the thing.

06 · How the firm is built

Expert-led. Designed so that judgment compounds.

Investment decisions are made by people with delivery records, and they carry final accountability for them. The firm's knowledge systems exist to make that judgment reusable: every technical review, project post-mortem and diligence process is captured and connected to what the firm already knows about the same technologies, suppliers, buyers and jurisdictions, so that each transaction makes the next one better underwritten.

The result is a firm that is deliberately small in its analyst layer and fully resourced where it must be — valuation, fund accounting, compliance, security and business continuity — with senior technical judgment applied to every position rather than to the largest ones.
