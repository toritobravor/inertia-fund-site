The path of a megawatt ↓
01 · Generation

After the physics works. Before a bank will lend against it.

Venture capital funds the physics. Project finance funds the proven. Between them sits the first commercial plant, the first factory, the first fleet — where the technology already works and the only remaining question is whether it can be built.

We judge a project on whether it can be built on time, not on how good the idea sounds.

02 · Transformation

Where we cannot see the transformer, we do not have a schedule.

The binding constraint has moved from the power plant to the grid connection: the transformer, the switchgear, and the permission to connect. A project's schedule is a procurement document, not an engineering one.

03 · Verification

Much of what is reported as scarcity is an announcement, not a commitment.

A shortage is only worth paying for if it is real and we have checked it ourselves: a signed order book, a quoted delivery date, a permit that has not been granted. We do not buy shortages we have read about.

2of 90 GW
On-site power announced for data centers, and the part of it that is actually running.
Roughly 90 GW across 59 projects has been announced; about 2 GW operates. The series moves with press releases — it was 56 GW four months earlier — so we treat it as directional, and we underwrite the megawatt that has a permit, a turbine slot and a signed buyer.
